StartBy Privacy Policy

Last updated 8 October 2026

StartBy works out when you need to start getting ready so that you arrive on time. To do that it needs to know where you are going and where you are setting off from. This page explains exactly what it collects, what leaves your phone, and what never does.

StartBy does not sell your data. There is no advertising in the app, and no advertiser, data broker or analytics company receives anything about you.

What stays on your phone

Almost everything. The following is stored on your device:

  • Your events — their names, addresses, times and reminders. The times of upcoming events are also shared with StartBy's server, as described under Location below
  • Your trip history — every departure and arrival StartBy has recorded, and the times it measured
  • What StartBy has learned about you: how long your journeys usually take, how long it takes you to park and walk in, how long you take to get out of the door
  • Your settings, and the name and number of anyone you have chosen to text

Your trip history and what StartBy has learned about you are not uploaded as such. StartBy's server receives only the times that result from them, and whether a route has been steady, as described under Location below. Addresses leave your phone only to work out a route, as described under Location below, or when you ask StartBy for directions and it opens your maps app with the address. Event names are not sent to StartBy's server.

Deleting the app removes all of this from your phone. Two things deliberately survive it: a small record in iOS's secure store — when your free trial started, and your Sign in with Apple identifier, email address and first name — so that reinstalling does not hand out a second trial, and whatever is held on the server, which is covered under Deleting your account and data below.

Location

StartBy asks for location access so it can time your journeys. What it does with that is limited and specific.

Working out a route. To measure a journey, StartBy sends the destination and any stops — as the address text or a map identifier — together with your starting point — the map point of your saved home address, or where your phone is — and the time you plan to leave, how you are travelling and whether you avoid tolls, to StartBy's own server. That server asks Google for a route and returns only the minutes and the destination's map point. The same happens when you search for an address: the text you have typed and your current location (or your saved home's map point) to search near. Google is never told who you are, and none of it is used for anything except answering the question you asked.

Rechecking traffic. A journey StartBy is watching is stored on its server, together with your account identifier and a random id for the event, as: two map points — where the journey starts from and the destination — the arrival and leaving times, whether you are driving, walking, cycling or taking transit, whether you avoid tolls, your time zone and language, how long the drive takes in minutes, and whether that route has been steady enough to stop checking. This is what lets the traffic be checked again while your phone is closed.

Nothing else about the event goes with it. Not its name, not the address as text, not who is going, not your calendar, and none of your trip history.

StartBy sends those points only once the journey is less than twelve hours away, and uses them only to check the traffic for that journey. StartBy automatically deletes watched journeys after they are more than 24 hours old during its daily cleanup.

Alerts the phone cannot hold. iOS limits how many alerts an app can keep waiting, so for upcoming events StartBy also gives its server the times it needs to send alerts on its behalf: when you need to arrive, leave, get ready and wake, which of those alerts the server should send, and whether you have already set off. These carry no map points, and no name or address.

Knowing you arrived. If you allow location access in the background, your phone alone works out when you have left and when you have arrived. That happens on the device. The result is a time, stored on your phone, and it is not uploaded. The only thing the server hears is that a journey has started, and so roughly when, so that it stops sending that event's alerts.

StartBy does not track you continuously. The only record of places you have been is your trip history — the destinations of journeys it timed — and that stays on your phone.

Your calendar

If you allow it, StartBy reads the titles, times and places of your upcoming events so you can choose which ones to be on time for — or, if you turn on calendar sync, brings them in for you. It does not read the notes. It looks at the guest list only to skip events you have declined and to leave events with other guests alone, and never stores or sends it.

StartBy can change one thing in your calendar: if you correct an event's time or place inside StartBy, it updates that event so the two do not disagree. It only ever does this for an event on your own calendar that has no other guests and does not repeat, because an event with guests is somebody else's meeting.

Your calendar is not uploaded. The address of an event you bring in is used the same way any other address is — to work out a route.

Contacts

If you ask StartBy to text somebody when you are running late, it opens your phone's own contact picker and receives only the one person you choose. StartBy never reads your address book.

Nothing is ever sent on your behalf. StartBy writes the message and opens it for you; you press send.

Signing in

StartBy uses Sign in with Apple. Apple gives the app an identifier for you, and your email address — the real one, or the private relay address Apple creates if you choose to hide it.

Both are stored on StartBy's server. The identifier is what makes your free trial follow you to a new phone, and it is what proves a request really comes from you rather than from somebody who has guessed your identifier. The email address comes with it as part of how Apple signs you in. Neither is used to contact you. The identifier is shared with RevenueCat so that a subscription follows you, as described under Payments; the email address is not shared with anyone.

Your name, if you share it, stays on your device. It is used to greet you, and to sign the running-late text StartBy writes for you.

You can stop sharing your email at any time in iPhone Settings, under your Apple Account.

Crash reports

If StartBy stops unexpectedly, or a request to its server fails, a report is sent to Sentry, a crash-reporting service, so the fault can be found and fixed. These reports contain technical information only: the error, where in the app it happened, and device details such as the iPhone model and iOS version.

They do not contain your addresses, your event names, your calendar, or your location. Web addresses are removed from every report before it is sent, in case one carries a place name. When a request to StartBy's server fails, the report says only that it failed, never where it was going or what was sent to it.

The support log

StartBy can show you a diagnostic log to help with a support question. It is built from your events and trip records, with the event names, addresses and notes StartBy can find replaced by [redacted] — check it before you send it. It leaves your phone only if you choose to share it, and only to whoever you send it to.

You can also report a problem from Settings. The report holds what you write, technical details — such as the app version, iOS version, iPhone model, language, which permissions are on, and timing figures for your next event — and, if you add one, a screenshot you pick. It opens in your own Mail app, share sheet or clipboard, and is sent only if you send it.

Sharing, screenshots and photos

You can share text, a link or one picture to StartBy from another app. The share extension saves it in a folder on your phone that only StartBy can read; StartBy turns it into a draft for you to check, and then removes the saved copy. A share it cannot read is set aside rather than imported. A shared link is kept as text and is not opened.

You can also pick one screenshot or photo, using iPhone's own photo picker, so StartBy does not ask for access to your photo library. The words in a picture are read on your phone, using Apple's built-in text recognition, and only those words are kept, until you save or discard the draft. StartBy removes its copy of a shared picture once the words are read, and does not keep a picture you pick.

If you choose Take a photo, StartBy opens iPhone's camera for one photo. iOS asks your permission the first time. The words in that photo are read on your phone in the same way, and the photo is not saved or uploaded.

When a picture looks like a schedule, StartBy may use Apple Intelligence, where it is available on your iPhone, to sort the words into rows before you review them.

Lock Screen and Apple Watch

From when it is time to get ready until you arrive, StartBy can show your journey as a Live Activity on your Lock Screen, with the event's name and its times. If you use the StartBy Apple Watch app, your iPhone sends the watch the name and times of your next event, and the watch keeps the latest copy.

Payments

If you subscribe, the payment is handled entirely by Apple. StartBy never sees your card details and never receives them.

StartBy uses RevenueCat to check whether a subscription is active. RevenueCat receives the identifier from your Sign in with Apple account and your purchase receipts, and nothing else about you. Their privacy policy is at revenuecat.com/privacy. Subscriptions are processed by Apple and by RevenueCat, our subscription service. Deleting your StartBy account does not cancel your App Store subscription; cancel it in the App Store.

Children

StartBy is not intended for children under 13 and does not knowingly collect anything from them.

Deleting your account and data

You can delete your account from inside the app, in Settings. This asks StartBy's server to delete your account and the data it holds for you — your identifier, your email address, your trial record, any journey being watched, the count of your recent route lookups, and the token used to send you notifications — and removes everything StartBy has stored on your phone except your display preferences, including any shared items waiting to be imported or set aside, any wake alarms, and the trial record that would otherwise survive deleting the app. If you use the Apple Watch app, the watch replaces its copy of your next event the next time your iPhone updates it.

If the server part cannot be completed, the app tells you and nothing is removed, rather than reporting success and leaving something behind. If your account is deleted but some data on the phone could not be cleared, the app tells you that too. If your phone is not signed in when you delete, StartBy clears the phone and says plainly that the server could not be checked — sign in once and delete again to clear it there too.

Sign in with Apple itself is revoked by you, in iPhone Settings under your Apple Account. No app is allowed to do that for you.

You can also write to the address below and ask for anything held about you to be deleted.

Who processes what

  • Supabase — hosts StartBy's server, which holds your Apple identifier and email address, your trial start date, the start and end points of journeys being watched, the alert times for your upcoming events, the notification token your phone was issued, and a count of how many route lookups you have made in the current hour. If you search for an address before signing in, StartBy creates an anonymous account on its server so lookups can be counted; signing in with Apple joins it to your account
  • Google Maps Platform — receives coordinates and addresses in order to return routes and address suggestions, and is never told who they belong to
  • Expo — delivers notifications sent from StartBy's server to your phone, and receives only the token that identifies the device and the words of the message. Expo also delivers app updates; when the app checks for one, it tells Expo technical details such as its version
  • Sentry — receives crash reports, as described above
  • RevenueCat — checks whether your subscription is active, and receives the identifier from your Sign in with Apple account and your purchase receipts
  • Apple — handles sign-in and any payment
